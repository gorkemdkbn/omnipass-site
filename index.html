One trip, three cards

Travel from Ankara to İstanbul to İzmir and you meet three cards, three apps and three balances. None of them talk to each other.

The idea

Think of it like a SIM card

Wherever you travel in Türkiye, your SIM keeps working. OmniPass does the same for public transport.

How a tap works

Four steps. One second

  1. 01

    Tap

    The card or NFC phone touches the validator.

  2. 02

    Verify

    The reader detects the card and checks it is real.

  3. 03

    Charge

    The correct fare for that city and mode is taken.

  4. 04

    Update

    The balance updates automatically. Gate opens.

The integration layer

One layer above every city

OmniPass does not replace local systems. Each city keeps its own validators, fares and ferries. OmniPass sits on top and records every journey.

Making it real

What makes OmniPass smarter

Phone as a card

NFC phones and smartwatches work like the physical card.

Discounts travel too

Student, teacher and senior fares work in every city. A student from Ankara pays the student fare in İzmir.

Visitor pass

A tourist card for foreign visitors, sold at airports and hotels.

Safe balance

The balance is tied to your account. Lose the card, keep the money.

Making it real

Who pays for it, and what could go wrong

Passengers

A one-time card fee. No extra cost per trip.

Cities

A small fee on each trip OmniPass processes. Cities also save: no local cards to print, no separate apps to build.

Partners

Banks, airports and tourism firms sponsor the visitor pass.

Exact fee levels would be set together with the pilot cities.

Cooperation81 municipalities must agree

Start with willing pilot cities and one national standard.

Old systemsEvery city is different

OmniPass sits on top. Cities keep their validators and fares.

CostNew software and validators

Upgrade step by step and share costs with partners.

SecurityData and offline payments

Encrypted cards, secure offline storage and regular checks.
